## 2. What was seeded, and why it looks like this

### Residents — 417 holdings across 7 blocks

**64% occupied, and the number is a choice.** A holiday complex on the Alanya
coast is not a city block: a large share of units are second homes that sit empty
most of the year, and 100% occupancy is the first thing a property manager would
disbelieve. Occupancy is graded by block — B01/B02 face the sea and fill first at
78/74%, B06/B07 completed later and sit at 48/44% — so the per-block panels look
like a building rather than a loop:

```
B01=75  B02=75  B03=65  B04=63  B05=52  B06=43  B07=44
```

271 owner-occupied, 146 let. Names are drawn from the four markets this complex
actually sells into (Turkish, German, Russian, British), because a resort in
Alanya with 415 German names would look wrong to the person being pitched.

**No generated resident carries a login.** They are people of record; only the
eleven seeded accounts can sign in. That is not tidiness — a generated profile
would widen a role's horizon and could make the owner-isolation test vacuous.

### 309 service tickets — full lifecycle, both SLA states

All 8 statuses, all 4 priorities, 10 categories. Weighted for what actually
breaks in a seafront building: `technical` 22% (air conditioning, lifts,
electrics) and `maintenance` 20% (plumbing, salt corrosion, balcony rails) lead;
`other` is 2%. 44% closed, with an open tail big enough to fill a board.

**41 of 309 breach their SLA** (13%), and the breach is real rather than a flag:
a ticket is breached when its resolution actually landed after `slaDueAt`.

Timestamps are causally ordered, because migration 06 has CHECK constraints on
exactly these pairs and a scattered generator produces rows the SQL seed would
refuse: `reportedAt <= slaDueAt`, `resolvedAt >= reportedAt`, `closedAt >=
resolvedAt`, and only terminal statuses carry those columns at all.

100 tickets are common-area and carry `unitId: null` — a lobby light belongs to
no dwelling.

### 12 months of ledger — 342 entries, per currency

Quarterly service charges for 50 units across four quarters, the payments against
them, the arrears that did not arrive (16% unpaid, unevenly spread so a few units
carry most of the debt), monthly common-area electricity in lira as a balanced
pair, and 54 vendor invoices with 57 payment movements.

Three currencies occur — EUR, TRY, USD — which is what makes a cross-currency
aggregate visibly wrong rather than plausibly wrong. Verified: **15 transaction
groups, 0 unbalanced, 0 spanning two currencies.**

Settlement is derived from the amount, never chosen beside it: no invoice claims
`paid` without `paid === total`, because `vendor_invoices_paid_status_agrees`
would refuse it. Same for overdraft: no wallet is negative without the flag, none
exceeds its limit.

### Documents, compliance, calendar, communications

151 documents — title deeds and purchase contracts per owned unit, handover
protocols, and a site certificate per month. **No file exists behind any of
them**: the buckets have never been created, `getSignedDocumentUrl()` already
returns `null` with a reason, and that stays. These rows demonstrate the
register, not a storage integration this build does not have. `checksumSha256` is
`null` for the same reason — a plausible-looking hash would be the one field in
the row that looks like proof.

20 compliance checks on the real regime (lift inspection 6-monthly, fire safety
annual, legionella 6-monthly, pool water monthly), some overdue, because the
point of the module is the ones that are.

222 activities across 13 months: weekly classes plus seasonal one-offs, with real
capacity and bookings that run against it, so `full` means booked === capacity
rather than being a label. Four events sit **after** the anchor deliberately — a
calendar with nothing upcoming is not a calendar.

67 threads hanging off **real tickets on real units**, 248 messages. A
communications module whose conversations reference nothing is a list of
paragraphs; the value is that a manager can read a complaint and open the ticket
it is about. Each thread's `messageCount` equals the messages actually emitted.

## 3. Determinism

No `Math.random()`, no `Date.now()`, no bare `new Date()`. Verified by scanning
all seven generator files with comments stripped, so the prose that *describes*
the ban is not counted as a use.

Randomness is a seeded mulberry32, and **each generator draws from its own named
stream**, seeded by an FNV-1a hash of the name. That is not decoration: with one
shared stream, adding a ticket shifts every later draw and the ledger, the
documents and the resident roster all change. Named streams mean a change to one
surface leaves the others byte-identical, which is what makes a snapshot diff
readable.

Every instant is `seedIso(dayOffset)` off the fixed `SEED_ANCHOR_ISO`
(2026-07-27), so **the demo does not age**: it is always the last twelve months
relative to the anchor, whatever today is.

```

     --test apps/web/lib/repository-contract.test.ts

      verified 53 seed builders are byte-stable
# tests 10   # pass 10   # fail 0
```

## 4. Every seeded record is visibly demo data

`PIVOT.md` §2 changes what may be seeded and leaves this standing.

Two channels, deliberately:

1. **The sync badge**, unchanged. Every repository reports `source: "local-seed"`
   and the dashboard says "Demo-Daten".
2. **`metadata.demo === true`** plus `metadata.generator = "demo-operations"` on
   every generated row. Page chrome does not survive a CSV export, a paste into
   an email, or a raw API read; this does.

Ids are shaped `xxx-dem0-4000-8000-000000000123` — uuid-shaped because the
columns demand it, with `dem0` in the middle so a row read out of a log or a
spreadsheet is identifiable without any surrounding context.

```
service_tickets        300/300 generated rows marked
activities             216/216
workforce_tasks        108/108
documents              141/141
compliance_checks        20/20
finance_ledger_entries 331/331
audit_events           220 rows, mark in beforeData/afterData
notifications           84 generated rows, mark in payload
```

**What is still not invented.** Nothing here claims anything about Azura World's
real business: no revenue, no occupancy rate, no real vendor relationship. The
buildings, the 656 units and the 188 rooms are harvested and real; the operations
on top of them are a demonstration and say so in every row.

## 6. Three defects the probe found, and what I did with each

None was found by reading. All three were in **pre-existing anchor data**, and
two of them were my own assertions being wrong rather than the data.

1. **`AZW-B03-0412` and `AZW-B07-0118` do not exist.** Blocks hold 94 units at
   most, so sequence 412 and 118 were never generated. Four documents and two
   communication threads pointed at them, and every drill-through from those rows
   to its unit was a 404. The ids predate the 94-per-block plan and survived
   because nothing joined the two seed modules until this branch populated both.
   **FIXED** — `SEED_UNIT_ID_OWNER` / `SEED_UNIT_ID_TENANT` repointed at real
   units. Five probe scripts pass the literal directly and do not read these
   constants, so they are unaffected; the string also survives as a prose example
   in a dozen comments, which is harmless.

2. **Three anchor activities sit after the anchor instant.** My assertion forbade
   it. The assertion was wrong: a calendar with nothing upcoming is not a
   calendar. Corrected, and an assertion added that the calendar *does* have
   future events.

3. **`AZW-T-0008` is cancelled, closed, and never resolved.** My assertion said
   `closedAt` implies `resolvedAt`. Wrong for cancellation: a cancelled ticket is
   withdrawn, not fixed. Corrected.
