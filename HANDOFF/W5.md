## 0. The previous verdict is withdrawn, and the new one is narrower and worse

The earlier 29 July run said the leading blocker was **M-001**: *"in a production build nobody can
sign in, so 20 of 24 dashboard pages are unreachable by anyone."*

**M-001 is fixed. I verified it by hand**, not by reading a handoff: `apps/web/.env.local` exists,
78 routes build, a wrong password reaches *"E-Mail-Adresse oder Passwort ist falsch."* rather than
*"nicht konfiguriert"*, and a correct password lands on `/de/dashboard`.

That is what made this session possible. **Passes 4, 5 and 7 have now genuinely run against the real
authentication path for the first time in this project**, with no access profiles
(`ENABLE_ACCESS_PROFILES=false`, and there is no role picker in this build).

What they found replaces one blocker with a worse one:

> **`lib/auth.ts:152` selects two columns that do not exist, so all eleven accounts are served a
> tenant's application** — and the consequence is that the 656-unit inventory renders **"0 von 0"**.

The database is correct throughout. RLS gives `admin` 656 units and `tenant` 23. The defect is one
line in the application layer, and **F1 already fixes it** — measured, not assumed.

## 5. A process finding, recorded because it nearly shipped a false result

My first F1/F2/F3 comparison reported that **F1 broke login entirely** — eleven blank dashboards and
"Die Anmeldung ist in dieser Umgebung nicht konfiguriert". **That was false**, and reporting it
would have killed a correct fix.

Port 3210 was held by a **stale `next start` from `D:\azura-w3h`**, another worktree. I was driving
someone else's build and attributing what it did to F1. I found it by resolving the PID that owned
the port, moved to 3221, confirmed the listener was `D:\azura-w5verify`, and re-ran. Every number in
§3 comes from the corrected run.

W3-C hit the identical trap on `qa:csp` and wrote it down. I hit it anyway. **Check the PID, not the
port** — hence test 16.
