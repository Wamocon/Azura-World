## 1. What each fix is

### M-003 — the `2.1x` was a division across two currencies

`239,171 USD ÷ 112,000 EUR = 2.135`. A conversion at an implied rate of exactly
1.0, printed two lines below the concierge's own sentence saying amounts in
different currencies are never converted.

**The fix is not a reworded sentence.** F-002's message is now **composed from
its own `competingValues`** by `compose_f002_message()` in
`scripts/build-azura-dataset.py`, immediately after the observations are
attached. It states a range per currency, and states a ratio **only within one
currency, naming it**. As shipped:

> The 1+1 entry price is unresolved across 19 observations from 6 publishers
> (Alanya-Home, Capital Estate, Haspo Realty, Housearch, Seaside Alanya, TERRA
> Real Estate). In EUR, the 13 observations whose publisher states a 1+1 layout
> run 112,000 to 310,000 EUR (Capital Estate, Haspo Realty, Seaside Alanya).
> That is a factor of 2.8 within EUR alone. In USD, the 2 observations whose
> publisher states a 1+1 layout run 238,967 to 239,171 USD (Housearch). Those
> differ by 0.1% within USD. The currencies are NOT converted and NOT compared
> to each other: no source in this dataset publishes an exchange rate or a rate
> date, so any ratio spanning them would be arithmetic on an invented rate. A
> further 4 rows are portal 'price from' entries whose publisher did not attach
> a layout, so they are carried but excluded from the ratio above: 125,000 EUR
> (Alanya-Home), 200,000 EUR (TERRA Real Estate), 220,000 EUR (Alanya-Home),
> 400,000 EUR (TERRA Real Estate). The causes compound: two currencies, no
> observation dates, different unit subsets, and 8 of 19 observations come from
> listings flagged stale (F-006).

`2.8` is `310,000 / 112,000` over the rows the publishers labelled `1+1`. It
agrees with the `2.77×` W3-C measured independently for its own `(nur EUR)`
badge, which is the first time those two figures have agreed.

The four `price from` rows are **carried and named**, not dropped: dropping them
would quietly narrow the spread the finding exists to show. They are excluded
from the ratio because their publisher did not say what they are a price for.

The em dash is gone from the message (it had one). Two things are **not** fixed
and are recorded in §7: the message is still English inside a German answer, and
still formats money anglo-style.

### M-004 — monthly rents were listed as asking prices

Traced. **`narrowListing()` in `apps/web/lib/ai-retrieval.ts` projected portal
rows into a local `ListingRow` and did not read `priceKind`.**

Everything upstream was correct. `scripts/sources.config.json` sets
`"priceKind": "rent"` on the Alto source with a note saying why; the builder
emits it on every row of `portalListings`; `azura-world-data.ts` states the rule
in a comment on the type — _"A monthly rent must never enter the sale-price
series — see F-002"_; the builder's own F-002 block filters on
`u.price_kind != "sale"`. The column existed, the rule was written down, and one
projection dropped it. `selectPrices()` then had nothing to filter on.

The two rows, confirmed as the only non-sale rows in all 47 listings:

| kind | amount | layout | size | publisher |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `rent` | 2,100 EUR | 1+1 | 70 m² | Alto Real Estate |
| `rent` | 1,000 EUR | 1+1 | 85 m² | Haspo Realty |

They are also the only two priced rows under EUR 100,000 anywhere in the set.

**A filter alone would not have been a fix** — the next projection would drop the
column the same way. So `PriceObservation` now carries a `unique symbol` brand
that only `saleObservation()` can apply. The type cannot be written as an object
literal anywhere, including inside `ai-retrieval.ts` itself, so **every
observation in a price series has passed the sale check**, and the check exists
in exactly one place.

`priceKind` narrows to `"sale" | "rent" | "unknown"`, and `"unknown"` is **not** a
sale. A dataset regenerated without the column empties the price series, which is
loud, rather than silently readmitting the rents.

**What is excluded is said out loud.** `describeExcludedRents()` adds, in all four
locales:

> 2 weitere Inserate zu dieser Wohnungsgröße sind Mietangebote und stehen nicht
> in dieser Preisspanne. Eine Monatsmiete ist kein günstiger Kaufpreis.

Removing the rows silently would have traded one honesty defect for another.

## 3. The gates, and the proof they reject

Four new rules in `scripts/verify-evidence.mjs`. They apply to **every** finding,
not only F-002: the defect class is "a sentence that says more than its own data
supports", and naming one finding leaves the other 23 unguarded.

| rule | refuses |
| --- | --- |
| `finding-cross-currency-ratio` | a multiplier in a message whose `competingValues` carry >1 currency, unless the multiplier's own sentence scopes it to exactly one |
| `finding-ratio-exceeds-record` | a stated ratio larger than the widest within-currency span in that finding's own record |
| `finding-publisher-count` | `across N publishers` where N is not the distinct publisher count in `competingValues` |
| `seed-cross-currency-ratio` | the same shape in `apps/web/lib/evidence-data.ts`, which is prose rather than data |

`finding-ratio-exceeds-record` recomputes rather than pattern-matching. A message
could satisfy the first rule by naming a currency beside a number that still came
from a cross-currency division; recomputing catches that.

### Each proved against the string that shipped

Both files patched in place, validator run, then restored and the restoration
verified byte-identical:

### The controls found four real holes and two false positives

Every one of these was found by running the gates, not by reading them. None
would have been visible in review.

1. **The pattern missed `a factor of 2.8` for want of an `x`.** The first version
   matched only `Nx` and `N-fach`, and `compose_f002_message()` writes "a factor
   of 2.8 within EUR alone". The gate would have skipped the exact sentence it
   exists to police; a corrupted build claiming `a factor of 9.9` passed with
   exit 0.
2. **The sentence splitter broke on decimal points.** Splitting on `.` cut "a
   factor of 2.8 within EUR alone" into "That is a factor of 2", which names no
   currency, so the gate reported its own correct message as a violation.
   Boundaries are `". "` now.
3. **A doubled backslash made the seed gate a silent no-op.**
   ``String.raw`\\b${code}\\b` `` compiles to "a literal backslash followed by
   b". It matched nothing, the gate reported success over a file that contained
   the defect, and the only reason it was caught is that the negative control
   failed. This is the failure mode this validator exists to prevent, in the
   validator.
4. **`-fach\b` did not match `2,1-fache`.** German inflects it, and
   `2,1-fache Spanne über vier Portale` is the exact string this project shipped
   in its German search title. The word boundary made the gate blind to the real
   instance.
5. **False positive: F-013.** The first publisher-count pattern matched any
   `<number> publishers` and fired on _"a stronger signal … than two publishers
   disagreeing"_, a rhetorical contrast, not a count. Narrowed to counting
   prepositions (`across|from|among|by|over`), which still catches M-010's
   `across four publishers` verbatim.
6. **False positive: the gate reported its own documentation.** The seed file
   explains the fix by quoting the sentence removed, and a scanner that treats
   any quoted run as a literal flagged that comment as a live overclaim.
   Comments are stripped first now, as `check-plain-language.mjs` already does.

A check that cannot tell a count from a comparison, or a comment from a string,
costs more than the drift it catches. That is `SECURITY-REVIEW.md`'s own
reasoning for narrowing its SEC-006 pattern, and it held here twice.

## 4. There were two F-002s, and that is why two reviews disagreed

`SECURITY-REVIEW.md` SEC-006: _"F-002 claims four publishers and carries
**three**."_
`MANUAL-TEST-REPORT.md` M-010: _"the `competingValues` array holds 19 entries
across **6** distinct publishers."_

**Both were right about the file they read.**

| file | owner | competingValues | distinct publishers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `apps/web/lib/azura-world-data.ts` | W0-B, generated | 19 | 6 |
| `apps/web/lib/evidence-data.ts` | W2-A, hand-written | 3 | 3 |

M-010 says _"Both the finding text and the security review are wrong about the
data."_ The truer statement is that there were two datas. The seed carried its
own `2.1x`, its own "across four publishers" beside three values, and a German
search title, `"F-002 · 1+1 Einstiegspreis: 2,1-fache Spanne über vier Portale"`,
repeating both. **Nothing checked it**: `verify-evidence.mjs` reads the emitted
artefact by design, and that design was right about the artefact and blind to the
seed.

All three strings are fixed, and `seed-cross-currency-ratio` now reads that file.

The seed's message states **its own** count and says it is a slice:

> The 1+1 entry price is unresolved. This seed carries 3 observations from 3
> publishers, a slice of the 19 in the full harvest: … Within EUR, the two
> observations here run 112,000 to 185,000 EUR, a factor of 1.7 within EUR alone.
> The USD figure is neither converted nor compared to them …

**`scripts/ai-probe.mjs` — probe #1 was asserting on the defect.** It required the
reply to contain `"112,000"` and `"239,171"` with anglo separators. Those strings
reached a German reply **only** because F-002's message stated them in English
prose, which M-003 lists as a rule violation in its own right. Once the amounts
came from `describePriceSpread`, which formats per locale, they render `112.000`
and `239.171` and the probe failed. The assertion now checks the German forms:
the same numbers, no longer found by way of a defective string. I also added an
`absent:` facility — the suite could only require strings to be **present**, which
is how it stayed green while the reply printed a cross-currency multiplier and two
rents. Probe #1 now asserts `2.1x`, `2,1-fach`, `2.100 EUR`, `1.000–190.000`,
`Alto Real Estate` and `four publishers` are all absent.

**`dashboard.listings.compare.findingNote` — my fix made N1's copy wrong.** That
note existed to warn the reader that F-002's `2.1x` compared a euro price with a
dollar one. It was correct and useful. Now that F-002 says no such thing, the note
described a claim that no longer exists, which is a new inaccuracy created by my
change. Rewritten in all four locales to say what F-002 now says. N1 should read
it.

   anglo-style (`112,000 EUR`). M-003 lists both as rule violations in the same
   string. All 24 findings are English; translating one would make the register
   inconsistent, and translating all 24 into four locales is a separate task with
   a different owner. The em dash — the one hard project rule of the three — is
   gone.
2. **`SECURITY-REVIEW.md` SEC-006 and SEC-007 still read OPEN**, and SEC-006 still
   states a publisher count that was never right for either file. That document is
   W4-C's record of what it observed; I have not rewritten another window's
   findings. **Request to W4-C:** mark SEC-006 and SEC-007 fixed by this branch, and
   correct SEC-006's count to name both files.
3. **`MANUAL-TEST-REPORT.md` is untouched** for the same reason. M-003, M-004 and
   M-010 are fixed; the report still lists them open.
4. **A title-plus-summary overclaim is not gated.** `seed-cross-currency-ratio`
   reasons about one string literal at a time. The seed's German search title held
   the multiplier while its amounts sat in a separate `summary` field, and the
   sweep would not have joined them. That instance was found by reading and is
   fixed; catching the shape needs the object graph this text-level check
   deliberately does not parse.
5. **F-013 carries one competing value, and it is a down-payment percentage.**
   `build-azura-dataset.py:1677` populates F-013 from
   `by_field["project.downPaymentPercent"]`, so a finding about two listing prices
   carries the value `30` attributed to Alanya-Home. The message is accurate; its
   record does not support it. Not in scope for this branch, found by the
   publisher-count gate's first false positive, and **it is the same class of
   defect as M-010** — a message and its record describing different things. Owner:
   W0-B.

## 9. Known gaps

- **`[GAP]` The gates cannot check a claim split across two fields.** §7.4.
- **`[GAP]` `finding-publisher-count` only reads English counting constructions**
  (`across|from|among|by|over N publishers`). A German or Turkish finding message
  stating a count would not be checked. All 24 findings are English today, so the
  gap is latent rather than live.
- **`[GAP]` No gate covers `SOURCES.md`.** SEC-007 records that the `2.1x` is
  quoted there too. It is a prose document, outside the validator's remit, and I
  have not read it end to end.
- **`[GAP]` The brand protects `ai-retrieval.ts`'s price series only.**
  `components/inventory/price-conflict-ladder.tsx` declares its own unrelated
  `PriceObservation`, and the evidence page fills it from
  `getPortalListings({ priceKind: "sale" })` — correct today, by an explicit filter
  rather than by the type. If that filter is ever dropped, nothing catches it.
- **`[GAP]` Invariant 6 is unverified in the committed state.** `sources/raw` is
  git-ignored (W0-B's open request #2), so on a fresh clone 1635 snapshot-hash
  lookups are skipped. I verified them once against the main tree's 96 snapshots;
  CI cannot.
